How to play the current build

A visual guide to the real game pages, expected decisions, and the instructor-led round cycle. Use the arrow keys or buttons to present; print to PDF for a handout.

1 · Sign in

Enter the game securely

What to do

  • Create an account the first time; otherwise sign in.
  • Use a separate browser profile/private window for each player during multiplayer play.

What to expect

The first local account becomes the instructor. Other accounts can join only after receiving a lobby code.

How to treat this page

This is access control, not a game decision screen. Do not share passwords; share the lobby code instead.

2 · Instructor lobby

Create the session and share the code

What to do

  • Select a phase deadline and create an instructor game.
  • Share the displayed join code with students.
  • Generate the starting map if prompted.

What to expect

Players appear here after joining. The game cannot start until the map has been persisted and seats are assigned.

How to treat this page

Use it as the classroom setup room. Keep the code visible only until everyone has joined.

3 · Seat assignment

Give every player a clear role

What to do

  • Assign Presidents to nations and Executives to companies.
  • Confirm every person has the intended role before starting.
  • Start the game once the roster is ready.

What to expect

Role permissions are enforced by the server. A President cannot submit company decisions, and an Executive cannot submit national policy.

How to treat this page

This is the source of truth for ownership. Explain that each seat has different private information and responsibilities.

4 · Instructor readiness board

Run the round without guessing

What to do

  • Watch the phase, deadline, and submission count.
  • Advance only when the required decisions are submitted—or the deadline has expired.
  • Advance through planning → presidential → company → processing.

What to expect

Late players receive conservative automatic submissions when the deadline is enforced. Phase changes and results refresh across connected clients.

How to treat this page

Use it as a facilitator control panel, not a player dashboard. It is where you pace discussion and keep the class synchronized.

5 · President dashboard

Steer the nation

What to do

  • Use Indexes to submit national policy during the presidential phase.
  • Use Intel & Military to set posture, military investment, and emergency preparedness.
  • Review financing, infrastructure, diplomacy, and published event outcomes.

What to expect

Budget choices have trade-offs. Preparedness is public recovery funding; insufficient coverage can create private recovery costs and lower approval/GDP after a disaster.

How to treat this page

Think like a government: balance resilience, civilian investment, and long-term national performance. Submit once the plan is defensible.

6 · Executive dashboard

Operate the company

What to do

  • Review Financials and Market conditions.
  • Use Sourcing to compare supply options and logistics.
  • Use Decisions to set price, production, staffing, R&D, and sourcing; save a draft, then submit in the company phase.

What to expect

Company choices are private before processing. Spending must be affordable, and submitted decisions feed the authoritative round resolver.

How to treat this page

Think like a business leader: protect cash, make a credible operating plan, and account for national conditions without assuming private government choices.

7 · Map and results

Read the shared world, then learn from the outcome

What to do

  • Open Map View from the top navigation to inspect the persisted game world.
  • After processing, review round results and Pangea Times articles.
  • Compare results with the decisions that were made; discuss cause and effect.

What to expect

All players receive the same public round result and news after processing. Event results expose public effects, not other players’ private pre-processing choices.

How to treat this page

The map is the shared context; results are the debrief. Ask: What happened? Why? What would we change next round?

Facilitator close

One-round checklist

  1. Instructor creates the game, shares the code, assigns seats, and starts.
  2. Instructor advances to the presidential phase; Presidents submit policy and readiness.
  3. Instructor advances to the company phase; Executives submit operating decisions.
  4. Instructor advances to processing; the server resolves the round once.
  5. Everyone reads the shared results and Pangea Times, then begins the next planning round.

Key expectation: Players submit intent; the server validates and resolves it. Reloading or reconnecting restores the canonical game state.
